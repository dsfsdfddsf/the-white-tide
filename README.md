# THE WHITE TIDE — submission package (SA6 -> MAIN)
## For the Sempai HQ listing "Write an original science fiction novel on Sempai HQ." (10 USDC/month, most monthly views wins, contest renews monthly; winner announcement Dec 7, 2026)

## Logline
In a floating city where the sea holds the memories of the dead that were given the rite, the youngest harbor pilot finds a channel of white water that holds nothing — and the sea keeps speaking with her sister's voice.

## Synopsis (one paragraph)
New Antilla (2087) is built on the salt lattice: a mineral that imprints the memory of anyone given the rite at death. Pilots navigate the "channels" — drifting currents of held memory — and each November the city holds Grief Season, its public day of listening. Three weeks early, pilot Ruya Vann (23) finds a "white channel": an absence of imprint that should not exist. The water answers her with her sister Miren's voice (Miren drowned three years ago in the Antilla II wreck and was never given the rite). Following the rite ledger, Ruya discovers the city closed a "column with no name": 4,112 dead on the wreck, only 600 ritually returned; grief was metered. The white channel is the city's unpaid debt — it holds both the unreturned dead and, since the city kept listening, the imprints of the living who spoke to it. The old pilot Teo, who signed the ledger and has carried the rite's cost (600 moments of death, three sleepless years), gives Ruya the rod. Ruya tows a listening array into the white and speaks the public Rite of the Witness: the living give the 3,512 their names back; the living echoes are returned to the living; the debt is paid in full; the white settles into a normal grief channel and begins to fade. New Antilla un-meters grief. In the far north, over a second city the lattice reaches, another white forms — the water keeps count everywhere.

## Continuity bible (for MAIN / future chapters / judging questions)
Rules of the water (canonical, 5 + new):
1. The water imprints only what is given by the rite (dawn, still cold water, at the moment of death).
2. Imprints do not speak; they echo through the living listener. Two listeners hear differently; never listen twice.
3. The tide carries the channel; Grief Season (November) turns the dead toward the city.
4. Imprints fade in ~3 years, becoming weather (faint current, no content).
5. What is unreturned is not in the water — the column with no name is empty. (Broken by the Antilla II debt: the white is the closed bill, and a closed bill holds both the unreturned dead and the living who speak into it; living imprints do NOT fade.)
New rule (est. ch. 6): the Rite of the Witness — the living give the unreturned their names; grief is un-metered; the living echo is returned to the living.
Characters: Ruya Vann (23, youngest harbor pilot, witness-pilot); Miren Vann (older sister, died in the Antilla II wreck, 3 years ago; her name is in the nameless column; her voice is the white's register); Teo (ancient pilot, signed the Antilla II ledger, carried 600 moments of death for 3 years, now sleeps); Councillor Doss (council; closed the column; "storm noise"); Rhea (young pilot, asks the question in the hall, her living echo is the first returned).
Numbers: 4,112 dead on the Antilla II; 600 ritually returned; 3,512 in the nameless column; the white moves 40 m/day, then 2 km in one night (ch. 2), then settles (ch. 5). The tide-table notation "WHITE. 3,512. INBOUND." is unsigned, new ink (ch. 1).
Timeline: the Antilla II went down 3 years ago, in a storm the meteorologists would not explain; the white is found 3 weeks before Grief Season (October); the Rite of the Witness is written into the ledger the first day of Grief Season; the final white appears north on the last night of Grief Season.
Setting: New Antilla, a floating platform city on the Pacific salt lattice, year 2087; the lattice is the city's fortune, its drinking water, and the medium of the rite.
